# Pain, workflow, and Jobs-to-Be-Done discovery

Use JTBD as a lens for circumstances and progress, not as a demographic persona exercise.

## Observe the job
Capture:
- actor and buyer (may differ);
- trigger/circumstance;
- desired progress/outcome;
- current sequence of steps;
- frequency and volume;
- failure consequence: money, time, risk, delay, lost revenue, cognitive load, compliance exposure, status/emotional cost;
- current alternative/workaround;
- switching forces: push from current state, pull of a new solution, anxiety of change, habit/inertia.

## Pain quality
Prefer pain that is:
- specific and observable;
- recurrent or high-consequence;
- owned by a named role;
- attached to a budget or costly workaround;
- worsened by scale/volume;
- poorly served despite active attempts to solve it.

Do not reject a non-recurring problem if consequence/ACV is high (for example, a rare but costly compliance event). Frequency and severity trade off.

## Opportunity statement
Write opportunities without embedding a solution:

> When [circumstance], [actor] needs to [progress/outcome], but [current friction/consequence] because [root cause/status quo].

Then branch into multiple solution wedges. This prevents "I found a pain, therefore my first implementation is correct."

## Discovery questions for real conversations
Ask about the past:
- "Walk me through the last time this happened."
- "What did you do next?"
- "What tools/people were involved?"
- "What did it cost in money or time?"
- "What happens if you do nothing?"
- "Who approves spend to fix this?"
- "What have you already tried?"

Avoid leading future-hypothetical questions as primary evidence.
