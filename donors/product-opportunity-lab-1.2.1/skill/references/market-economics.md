# Market economics, pricing, and distribution

## Three market views

Keep these separate:
1. **Reachable beachhead** — accounts/users the founder can plausibly reach in the next 6–18 months.
2. **Serviceable market** — plausible segment under current product/geography/channel constraints.
3. **Market ceiling** — broader long-run opportunity if the model works.

For `cashflow`, beachhead economics dominate. For `venture`, ceiling/growth/timing matter more. Top-down TAM never rescues a weak beachhead.

## Bottom-up model

Use one of:
- `reachable accounts × plausible annual contract value`;
- `reachable customers × jobs/year × plausible revenue per job`;
- another unit model that matches how buyers actually pay.

Show ranges and assumptions rather than one precise number.

## Existing value flow

Capture current cost/value from:
- software subscriptions;
- labor/FTE time;
- agency/BPO/freelancer spend;
- error/rework/downtime/delay;
- compliance/risk exposure;
- revenue leakage or conversion loss;
- inventory/capital cost;
- opportunity cost when reasonably measurable.

Existing cost is a pricing anchor, not automatic willingness to switch.

## Price corridor

Estimate:
- **floor:** cost to deliver + required margin/support burden;
- **reference:** current alternative/status-quo cost;
- **ceiling:** buyer-perceived value or budget limit;
- **test price:** price that makes the commitment experiment informative.

Avoid arbitrary "AI SaaS" pricing.

## AI/service-as-software economics

Model:
- model/API/compute cost;
- human-in-loop and exception rate;
- retry/quality-control cost;
- onboarding/integration cost;
- support load;
- data/storage/security overhead;
- refund/SLA/error exposure where relevant.

Headline inference cost alone can make an unprofitable workflow look attractive.

## Distribution and adoption

For finalists specify:
- exact ICP list/source/community;
- reachable qualified target set sized for the next discriminating test (often ~10–20 for founder-led low-ACV discovery, but smaller enterprise samples or larger consumer/self-serve samples may be appropriate);
- user vs buyer vs budget owner;
- trust/credibility requirement;
- sales motion: self-serve, founder-led, partner/channel, enterprise procurement;
- likely cycle and implementation friction;
- switching trigger;
- expected acquisition effort or CAC proxy.

## Sensitivity test

Identify the 2–3 assumptions that dominate economics, such as price, conversion, human exception rate, retention, or sales effort. Show downside/base/upside ranges. If the decision flips on a small change in one weakly evidenced parameter, that parameter becomes a validation priority.
