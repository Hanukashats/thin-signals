# Founder / CEO red-team interrogation

Run this after initial scoring, before proof-of-commitment design. The goal is to make the thesis harder to believe unless evidence survives.

## Forcing questions
1. **Demand reality:** Which target actor has enough pain/value at stake to change behavior now? What evidence distinguishes "reasonable" from "urgent"?
2. **Status quo:** What exactly happens today? Why has the user not already solved it?
3. **Actor specificity:** Who uses, who suffers, who approves, who funds/resources it, and who can block adoption? For commercial opportunities, identify the payer/budget owner.
4. **Narrowest wedge:** What is the smallest complete outcome worth the target actor adopting, authorizing, funding, or contributing to for this archetype?
5. **Observation:** Have we seen this workflow/pain happen, or inferred it from market content?
6. **Why now:** Which external change creates a genuine timing advantage?
7. **Switching/adoption trigger:** What event makes the target actor leave or augment the current solution despite inertia, migration, or coordination cost?
8. **Distribution:** Identify a reachable set of qualified target actors large enough for the next discriminating test, plus a credible contact/adoption route. The required count depends on archetype, ACV, channel, and decision; if access is weak, state that explicitly.
9. **Trust barrier:** What must be true for the responsible actor to let this system touch data, workflow, money, reputation, production systems, or contributors?
10. **Failure precedent:** Who tried something adjacent and failed/pivoted? What is materially different now?
11. **Commoditization:** If frontier models improve and prices fall, does this product become stronger or become a feature?
12. **Falsifier:** What single finding would make us stop pursuing this immediately?

## Material step-change test
Do not demand a literal 10× improvement. Ask whether the wedge creates a **salient enough improvement or risk reduction to overcome adoption friction** in at least one actor-relevant dimension (cost, speed, risk, convenience, quality, revenue, compliance, developer experience, operational load) while keeping the others acceptable.

## Red-team output
- strongest surviving thesis;
- top 3 reasons it may fail;
- evidence that addresses each;
- unresolved fatal assumption;
- next test with highest decision-changing information value.
