# Competitors, substitutes, status quo, and failure archaeology

## Map the real competitive set

1. **Direct products** — same buyer/job/outcome.
2. **Adjacent/substitute tools** — solve part of the job or solve it differently.
3. **Services/people** — agencies, consultants, BPO, freelancers, internal staff.
4. **Status quo / do nothing** — spreadsheets, email, manual coordination, tolerated risk.

A competitor-free category can indicate whitespace, weak demand, hidden services competition, or repeated category failure. Investigate before celebrating it.

## Competitor record

Capture when material:
- URL/current status and verification date;
- target segment and buyer;
- promised outcome;
- pricing/model if public;
- workflow depth/integrations;
- distribution clues;
- traction proxies, explicitly labeled as proxies;
- strengths;
- recurring complaint clusters;
- switching costs;
- current trajectory.

## Failure archaeology without causal overreach

A shutdown is not automatically a market failure. Classify the outcome first:
- `FAILED/CLOSED`
- `PIVOTED`
- `ACQUIRED`
- `SUNSET_BY_PARENT`
- `DORMANT`
- `OPEN_SOURCE_ABANDONED`
- `UNKNOWN`

Then classify the causal claim:
- `VERIFIED` — founder/company/public record directly states the cause or strong data demonstrates it;
- `SUPPORTED_HYPOTHESIS` — multiple independent facts point to the cause;
- `SPECULATIVE` — plausible but not evidenced.

Do not write "failed because there was no market" unless the causal evidence supports that claim.

Investigate mechanisms such as:
- weak problem/budget;
- buyer/user mismatch;
- expensive acquisition or procurement;
- integration/implementation burden;
- weak retention/low recurrence;
- trust/compliance blockers;
- poor unit economics or hidden services load;
- commoditization/incumbent bundling;
- platform dependency/policy change;
- enabling technology not ready;
- timing/capital/runway/execution issues unrelated to demand.

## Gap test

A missing feature is not a market gap. A useful gap requires:
`valuable job + recurring/high consequence + dissatisfied status quo + switching trigger + reachable buyer + viable delivery`.

Compare failed analogs with surviving competitors to avoid survivorship bias in either direction.
